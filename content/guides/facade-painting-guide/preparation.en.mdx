If there's one lesson you learn after enough facade repaints, it's this: the result comes down 70% to preparation and only 30% to the paint itself. An expensive paint on a poorly prepped surface will peel within two years; an affordable paint on a properly prepped surface can last a decade.

The first step is always a high-pressure wash, which removes dust, mold, moss and any loose or flaking paint. Skip this step and the new paint just sits on an unstable base and will peel away along with it. After washing, the surface needs plenty of drying time — usually 48 hours in good weather — before moving on to the next step.

Next comes crack repair, and material choice matters a lot here. We use flexible filler compounds specifically designed to move with the building's expansion and contraction, instead of rigid materials that will crack again at the same spot within a few months. On surfaces that have chalked heavily or are particularly porous, we apply a penetrating primer (fixative) before the finish coat, to bind the loose dust and ensure even adhesion.

Finally, on older buildings it's worth checking for active moisture before any painting starts. If there's a sign of rising damp or a leak, painting needs to wait until the underlying problem is fixed — otherwise the new paint will bubble at that exact spot within a few months, no matter how good its quality.
