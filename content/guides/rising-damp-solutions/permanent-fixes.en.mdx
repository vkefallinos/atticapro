There are several ways to stop rising damp, and the right choice depends on the building's age, wall thickness and budget. There's no single "one size fits all" solution — there's the right solution for a specific building.

The most common method today is creating a chemical damp-proof course: a specialized liquid (usually siloxane or silane-based) is injected under pressure into a series of holes drilled along the base of the wall. The material diffuses through the material's pores and, once cured, forms an impermeable layer that stops moisture climbing any further — essentially an artificial barrier where no membrane ever existed.

On walls with severe or long-standing damage, it's often better to remove the old, affected plaster to a height of at least 1 metre above the visible damp line and replace it with specialized breathable renders (a "re-plastering" system). These renders don't stop moisture from being present inside the wall, but they let water vapor escape outward without bubbling or peeling, while trapping the salts before they reach the surface.

In extreme cases — old buildings with very thick stone walls or severe ground moisture — we combine both methods along with external foundation waterproofing where that's feasible. The cost of a typical chemical barrier treatment on a 10-linear-metre wall usually runs to a few hundred euros, while a full re-plastering restoration costs more but delivers a cosmetic refresh alongside the fix.
