The most deceptive thing about rising damp is how slowly it develops — often for years — before it becomes obvious enough to worry anyone. The earlier you catch it, the smaller the damage, and the cheaper the fix.

The first sign is usually plaster that's peeling or bubbling near the base of a wall, typically up to about 80-100cm high. Plaster bubbles because moisture moving through the masonry dissolves salts naturally present in the building material and carries them toward the surface. As the water evaporates, those salts crystallize behind the plaster layer, building up pressure until it detaches.

A little later, white, salty crusts appear on the surface — what tradespeople call "efflorescence." Touch it and you'll immediately notice it's different from ordinary dust or dirt. Around the same time, many occupants report a persistent musty smell in ground-floor or basement rooms, even before any damage is visible — the nose often warns you before the eye does.

In more advanced stages, moisture reaches wooden elements — skirting boards, door frames, floors — causing warping, dark staining, or even rot. Metal fittings near floor level (nails, screws, furniture frames) start rusting faster than you'd expect. If you recognize two or more of these signs in your space, it's worth getting a professional inspection before the problem works its way deeper into the structure.
