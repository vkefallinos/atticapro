There's no single "best" waterproofing system — there's the right system for a given roof, depending on whether it sees foot traffic, its slope, and whether it has lots of pipework or unusual details.

Elastomeric bituminous membranes remain the most battle-tested solution for roofs with regular foot traffic, like those used as terraces. They're installed in sheets with heat-welded joints and offer excellent mechanical durability, with a real-world lifespan of 15 to 20 years when properly installed. Their downside is the seams between sheets, which demand careful work from an experienced installer.

Liquid polyurethane waterproofing is applied by roller or brush in multiple coats, forming one continuous membrane with no seams at all. That makes it ideal for roofs with lots of pipework, irregular geometry, or many details around parapets — spots where sheet membranes struggle. It's also the easiest solution to repair locally down the line, since you can simply add a fresh coat to that one spot.

Cool roof coatings aren't a complete waterproofing system on their own — they're a reflective finish coat applied over an already-waterproofed surface. Thanks to their high reflectivity to solar radiation, they can lower the slab's temperature by up to 20°C on hot days, with a direct impact on the air-conditioning costs of the room below. We usually recommend them as a supplement, not a substitute for genuine waterproofing.
