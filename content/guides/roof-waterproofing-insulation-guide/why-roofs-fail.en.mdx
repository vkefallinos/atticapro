Most flat roofs in Attica were built in the '70s, '80s and '90s, with plain cement screed or bituminous felt as the only waterproofing. Both materials have a realistic lifespan of 10 to 15 years — not because they're poor quality, but because the roof is the most stressed surface on any building.

That stress comes from three directions at once. First, sunlight (especially UV) gradually breaks down the bonds in bituminous or cementitious materials, making them brittle. Second, sharp temperature swings — from 60°C at midday in July to 5°C on a winter night — cause expansion and contraction that fatigue the material as if you were bending it back and forth continuously. Third, rainwater pooling in areas with insufficient slope has all the time it needs to work its way into every hairline crack the first two factors have already created.

The result is a vicious cycle: hairline cracks widen with every rain-dry cycle until they're large enough to let water through the slab. From there, water travels through the concrete's reinforcement, eventually reaching the ceiling of the floor below — often quite far from where the leak actually started, which is what confuses many owners when they go looking for the cause.

A roof's useful life isn't the same for every building. Orientation, shading, the quality of the original construction, and whether the roof sees foot traffic (used as a terrace, say) all significantly affect how quickly the first problems show up.
